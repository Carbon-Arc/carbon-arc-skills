# Reader: talent representation

Read at the start of every run. This file is what makes this package speak to the people who represent
talent; the rest of the library is the same for every reader.

## Who the reader is

**The talent's own team**: management, agency or label. Not a brand buying an endorsement, not a
promoter, not an investor. They know the artist's own numbers (royalty statements, tour settlements,
socials dashboards) far better than any panel. They see the whole conversation.

## The framing principle: state it in every module

**Carbon Arc sees the talent's audience. It does not see the talent's income.** Streams, followers,
playlist position, brand affinity and resale tickets are reach and attention. None of them is revenue.
Royalty rates, advances, publishing splits, guarantees, merch, sync fees and endorsement money are all
invisible, at every grain.

Where Carbon Arc is better than the team's own numbers is everything **relative**: whether a flat
quarter is the artist or the whole cohort, which peers are pulling away and on what basis, who the
audience actually is, and which brand partnerships are distinctively this artist's versus the ones every
artist in the lane can claim. **Every module answers a relative question.** A number with no benchmark
beside it is not a finding.

**The peer set is the yardstick.** Talent has no disclosed comps and no consensus estimate, so the peer
band derived in the setup (`references/setup-talent.md`) is the whole benchmark layer. It is built from
the data, shown to the reader first, and every later figure is read against it.

Consequences, not negotiable:
- **Reach is never revenue.** Never phrase a stream count, follower count or affinity score as, compare
  it to, or convert it into money. Say so once, in full, on Sources & method.
- **No advice about the talent's business or worth.** No valuation, no "you should sign, tour or take
  this deal", no fee guidance. The brief supplies audience evidence; the team makes the call.
- **Reputational restraint.** Talent are real people. Report audience and commercial signal. Include a
  personal, legal or relationship event only where the reader needs it to read a data movement, stated
  neutrally in one sentence and sourced. If a movement has no defensible public explanation, say the
  cause is unknown.
- **Backtest.** There is no reported figure the panel is trying to reproduce, so there is no backtest
  page. `ca-core` gate 8 still applies: check one panel level per asset against a published figure (a
  box-office gross, a chart or certification figure) before quoting any level.
- **Never reproduce lyrics**, in any form, and quote at most one short line of press per callout.

## Vocabulary

Music and film business terms are fine: *streams*, *plays*, *routing*, *on-sale*, *resale*, *sync*,
*catalog*, *box office*, *streaming window*. *Index*, *cohort* and *over-indexes* are fine with a
one-line gloss the first time. Tab labels are phrased as a manager would ask the question: *"Is anyone
in my lane pulling away?"*, not *"Momentum"*.

Also banned for this reader, on top of the shared list: *PMI*, *nPMI*, *co-occurrence score*, and
insight numbers. Say *"artists who share the same playlists"* or *"shared-audience strength"*.
