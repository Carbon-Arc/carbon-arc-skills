Has Brand A recovered from the outbreak?

It won back 60% of the gap, then stalled

Card transactions fell 33% in the week ending Jul 24, 29 points worse than other quick-service restaurants. By late August the gap had narrowed to about 11 points, and it has not closed further in five weeks.

What's behind the answer

6 findings
01

The gap narrowed to about 11 points, then stopped closing

From a −29-point trough, Brand A's gap to the category recovered steadily through August. It has sat near −11 points for five straight weeks, well outside the normal-week range.

02

The loss is guests, not price

Q3-to-date spend of −15% splits into transactions −18% and ticket +2.9%. Ticket slowed from +5.0% in Q2, consistent with $1 value offers pulling mix down.

03

The damage was national, not regional

Two thirds of the trough loss landed outside the five first-implicated states. Those states have since come back in line: −1.3 points vs the rest of the US over the last four weeks.

04

The category slowed too, but explains only about 4 points

Other QSR brands went from +1.2% in June to −4.3% in Q3. That leaves about 14 of the 18-point decline on the outbreak. Brand D's 2-point edge is too small to show customers switching.

05

Management's July figure is far milder than the card panel

Management put US same-store sales at −2% through Jul 27; card spend over the same days fell 15%, though the panel tracked Q2 closely. We cannot reconcile the two, so nothing here is a forecast of reported comps.

Data limit

A panel skew is unlikely: the drop appears in every state, in store and online, and a third-party visits panel also reads double digits. Card data misses cash and delivery-app orders, but neither plausibly closes a 13-point gap.

06

One event explains the quarter

We tested five candidates. Only the outbreak cleared the normal-week range; the rest are calendar effects or too small to see in weekly data.

Bottom line

Brand A exits the quarter's data about 11 points behind the category, with no improvement since late August. Q4 opens with most of the gap still to close.

Method

  • Subject is the Brand A brand, not the parent company's ticker. Card spend maps to US system sales, not parent-company revenue.
  • Q3 is Jul 1–Sep 30; the panel covers Jul 4–Sep 18, 77 of 92 days. The partial week ending Sep 25 is dropped.
  • YoY compares each Sat–Fri panel week with the week 52 weeks earlier. Ticket = spend ÷ transactions.
  • Control is QSR minus Brand A. Outbreak effect = the gap to control, less its +0.3-pt average over Jan 16–Jul 3.
  • Normal-week range is ±3.3 pts (2σ of 25 pre-outbreak weeks). The outbreak accounts for −14 pts of −18% (95% band −18 to −10).
  • No estimate of reported sales is given. Channel and age cuts were not run.
