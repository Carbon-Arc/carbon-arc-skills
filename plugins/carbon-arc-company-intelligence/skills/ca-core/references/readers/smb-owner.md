# Reader: small-business owner

Read at the start of every run. This file is what makes this package speak to the owner of a small or
regional business reading about its category and its markets; the rest of the library is the same for
every reader.

## Who the reader is

**The owner or leadership of a small or regional business**: a single location, or a local or regional
operator with a handful to a few dozen sites. They run the business; most are not analysts. They see the
whole conversation.

The range is wide, and no vertical is the default. Examples of the range, not a fixed list: a restaurant
or coffee shop, a salon or spa, a fitness studio, a dental or veterinary practice, a car wash or repair
shop, a furniture or hardware store, a dealer, a contractor, a hotel or marina. Most do not appear in the
data by name, and the report must still serve them.

## The framing principle: state it in every module

**You know your own sales, your own customers and your own costs better than any panel ever will. What
the panel sees is everyone else**: businesses like yours elsewhere, your category, the market around you,
and what the same months did where you are not. **Every module points outward.** It points inward only
far enough to place the business against that outside, and it hands the comparison with the owner's own
numbers back to the owner.

The questions this reader brings, and the module that answers each:

| The question | Module |
|---|---|
| Is what I'm feeling happening to everyone in my business, or just me? | `ca-category` |
| Is the customer behind my category getting stronger or weaker, and for whom? | `ca-macro` |
| How is the market around me doing, and which nearby markets are coming back first? | `ca-markets` |
| Is my wage and cost pressure local, or happening everywhere? | `ca-local-costs` |
| What did a promotion, a price change or an event actually do? | `ca-events` |

Consequences, not negotiable:

- **The business is never measured unless it is in the data.** Where it is not, no sentence, chart title
  or callout may imply that a panel observed it. Do not write "your traffic", "your revenue", "your
  customers" or "you are underperforming"; the subject of every sentence is the market, the category or
  a named comparable business. Where it is in the data, say once at which grain, and never read it below
  that grain.
- **No diagnosis.** The report does not say whether the business is doing well or badly, or attribute
  anything to how it is run. Where the natural next step is a comparison with the owner's own figures,
  hand it over: *"if your ticket is still rising faster than this, that is a gap worth checking in your
  own books"* is allowed; *"you are overpricing"* is not.
- **Name the report for the business; never claim its performance.** The business is the subject of the
  report even when it is not the subject of the measurement. *"Acme's markets grew"* is fine, because the
  markets came from the owner; *"Acme grew"* is a measurement claim and is not.
- **Never present a panel figure as anyone's sales, market share or revenue.** One basis statement, once.
- **The panel never says how a purchase was paid for.** Card data records a payment, not its terms; do not
  state or imply anything about financing, credit or payment plans.
- **No advice the owner should get from a licensed professional or make themselves.** No tax, accounting,
  lending or investment recommendation, and no "you should open, close, expand or raise prices". The
  report supplies market evidence; the owner decides.
- **No backtests in the deliverable.** There is no reported figure being reproduced. Where a business or
  a comparable is listed, the `ca-core` §3 contradiction check still runs silently.

## Vocabulary

Plain business English, short sentences. *Sales*, *customers*, *visits*, *average ticket*, *year over
year*, *trade area*, *market*, *season*, *wages* are fine. *Index* and *adjusted for the panel's age mix*
are fine with a one-line gloss the first time. Say *businesses like yours* or *comparable businesses*,
never *analogs* or *analog brands*. Avoid *cohort* (say *age group*), *debiased* (say *adjusted*),
*diff-in-diff* and *control group* (say *against comparable markets that were not affected*). Tab labels
are phrased as an owner would ask: *"Is it everyone, or just me?"*, not *"Category health"*.

Also banned for this reader, on top of the shared list: *CBSA* and *MSA* (say *metro area*), *DMA* (say
*media market* if it must appear), *NAICS* (say the industry in words), *resident wallet* (say *what
people living here spend*), *catchment* (say *trade area*), and any insight number or dataset code.
